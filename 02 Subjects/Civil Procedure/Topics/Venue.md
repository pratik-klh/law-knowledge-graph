# Venue

The third side of the [[Jurisdiction Triangle]]: the proper **district** for the suit. Venue requirements are **statutory**, so be familiar with them.

## Proper venue: [[28 USC 1391 - Venue|28 U.S.C. §1391]]
- **(b)(1)** If all Ds reside in the same state: any district where **any D resides**.
- **(b)(2)** Where a **substantial part of the events** giving rise to the claim occurred, or where the **property** at issue is located.
- **(b)(3)** Fallback: if neither fits, any district where any D is subject to the court's PJ for this action.
- **Residence:**
  - Individuals: where **domiciled** (§1391(c)(1))
  - Corporations: any state where they are subject to **PJ** (§1391(c)(2))
  - Domestic and alien defendants together: the suit may be brought in any district where a D resides (§1391(c)(3))
- [[Bates v. C & S Adjusters]]: venue can be proper in **multiple** districts.

## When venue is wrong or inconvenient
- [[Transfer of Venue]]: §1406 (improper venue) and §1404 (convenience)
- [[Forum Non Conveniens]]: usually leads to **dismissal** in favor of a foreign forum
- [[Forum Selection Clauses]]: enforced unless unfair or unreasonable

## Takeaways
- A judge can **dismiss** for improper venue or **transfer** in the interest of justice to a proper court.
- A court can also transfer for **convenience**, weighing various factors.
- Choice-of-law rules after transfer keep parties from manipulating the system.
