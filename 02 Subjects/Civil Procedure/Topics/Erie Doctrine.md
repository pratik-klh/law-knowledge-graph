# Erie Doctrine: Ascertaining the Applicable Law

**Question:** what substantive law should federal courts apply when deciding **state-law claims**?
- **Federal question cases:** obviously federal law, since the suit is brought under the Constitution or an Act of Congress.
- **Diversity** or **supplemental** jurisdiction over a state-law claim: this is where Erie applies.

## Timeline
1. [[Rules of Decision Act]]: "the laws of the several states" are the rules of decision
2. [[Swift v. Tyson]] (1842): federal courts may apply "general" common law
3. [[Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins]] (1938): **state substantive law, federal procedural law**
4. [[Guaranty Trust Co. v. York]] (1945): [[Outcome Determinative Test]]
5. [[Byrd v. Blue Ridge Rural Electric Cooperative]] (1958): [[Byrd Balancing Test]]
6. [[Hanna v. Plumer]] (1965): if a valid FRCP covers the point, apply the FRCP
7. [[Walker v. Armco Steel Corp.]] (1980): Hanna applies only with a **direct collision**
8. [[Stewart Organization v. Ricoh Corp.]] (1988): federal **statutes** are read broadly and win under the Supremacy Clause
9. [[Klaxon Co. v. Stentor Electric Manufacturing]] (1941): apply the forum state's **conflict-of-law** rules

## Federal vs state claims
The law federal courts apply to claims within their SMJ can be federal (arising under federal law) or state (arising under state law).
