# Work Product Doctrine (Rule 26(b)(3))

- **Trial-preparation materials** prepared by or for a party or its attorney **in anticipation of litigation** are protected.
- Discoverable only on a showing of **substantial need** **and** inability to get the substantial equivalent **without undue hardship**. Classic example: the witness has died, and only the attorney's interview notes remain.
- Even then, the attorney's **mental impressions, conclusions, opinions, and legal theories** stay protected and can be redacted.
- **Qualified**, not absolute. It's broader than [[Privileges|attorney-client privilege]] because it covers materials, not just communications. The exception doesn't reach materials also covered by attorney-client privilege.
