# Protective Orders (Rule 26(c))

A written court order **restricting or prohibiting** discovery. Any recipient of a discovery request or subpoena may move for one.

- **Where:** the court where the case is pending, or the district where discovery will happen (e.g., a deposition).
- **Standard:** **good cause**. The discovery is annoying, embarrassing, oppressive, or unduly burdensome or expensive.
- **Meet and confer first:** the movant must confer, or try to, in good faith, and certify that in the motion.

## What the court can order
No discovery at all · specified terms (time, place, cost allocation) · a different discovery method · limited scope · who may be present · a sealed deposition · confidential commercial or R&D information revealed only in a certain way · simultaneous filing in sealed envelopes

## Expenses
If granted, the court may make the party who sought discovery pay the movant's reasonable expenses, including attorney's fees, unless that would be unjust (e.g., the movant never conferred in good faith).
