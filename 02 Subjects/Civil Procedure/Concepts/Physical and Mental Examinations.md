# Physical and Mental Examinations (Rule 35)

## Requirements
The court may order a **party** (or a person in a party's custody or control) to submit to an exam if:
- their physical or mental condition is **in controversy**,
- there is **good cause**, and
- notice is given to all parties and the examinee.

A **court order** is required; a mere notice isn't enough. A party told to produce a person in their custody need only make **good-faith efforts**.

## The order
Must specify **who** performs the exam and its **time, place, manner, conditions, and scope**. The examiner must be suitably **licensed or certified** (not necessarily in the court's state).

## Reports
- On request, the movant gives the examinee the report, plus reports of other exams of the **same condition**. The examinee must then provide similar reports of the same condition.
- The written report includes findings, diagnoses, conclusions, and test results.
- **Requesting a report waives** doctor-patient privilege for all exams of that condition (see [[Privileges]]).
- Failure to exchange reports leads to a **motion to compel** and sanctions, up to **excluding medical testimony**.
