# Counterclaims (Rule 13)

A claim the **defendant** has against the **plaintiff**. It goes in the [[Answer]].

## Compulsory (13(a)) vs permissive (13(b))
A defendant always **can** bring a counterclaim, but **must** (or risk losing it) if it arises out of the **[[Same Transaction or Occurrence]]** as P's claim.

### Compulsory counterclaims
- Must arise out of the same transaction or occurrence as the opposing claim
- Must **exist at the time** the original claim is brought
- Doesn't require adding new parties over whom there's no PJ or SMJ
- **SMJ:** comes through [[Supplemental Jurisdiction]] ([[United States v. Heyward-Robinson Co.]])

### Exceptions (13(a)(2)): no need to bring it if
- The counterclaim is **already pending** in another lawsuit
- The original claim is based on [[In Rem Jurisdiction|in rem]] or [[Quasi in Rem Jurisdiction|quasi in rem]] jurisdiction (PJ extends only to the property, not to a new claim)

### Permissive counterclaims (13(b))
Any counterclaim that isn't compulsory. Needs its **own** SMJ basis.
