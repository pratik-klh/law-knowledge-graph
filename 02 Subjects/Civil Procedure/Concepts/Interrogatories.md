# Interrogatories (Rule 33)

- Written questions to **another party** (not non-parties). Scope is the same as Rule 26.
- **Limit:** **25**, including discrete subparts, unless the court allows more. Subparts on the same theme count as one; discrete subjects count separately (no bright-line rule).
- Organizations answer through an **officer or agent**.
- **30 days** from service to answer or object (can be changed by stipulation or court order).
- Answers are **under oath** and signed by the person answering. Objections are signed by the **attorney**.
- Objections must be **specific, written, and timely**, or they're waived (unless good cause).
- **Business-records option (33(d)):** if answering requires examining records and the burden is the same for either side, identify the records and let the requester inspect and copy them.
- Answers can be **used at trial** like live testimony.

## Strategy
- **Uses:** identify witnesses, force disclosure of trial witnesses, narrow the scope.
- **Downsides:** burdensome, can reveal your theory, invite reciprocal interrogatories, and answers are often evasive and lawyer-drafted.
- Interrogatories generate the **most objections and motions** of any discovery device. The order of methods is flexible; depositions often come first.
