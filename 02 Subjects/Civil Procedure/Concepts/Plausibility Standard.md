# Plausibility Standard

From [[Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly]] (2007) and [[Ashcroft v. Iqbal]] (2009).

- To survive a motion to dismiss, a complaint must contain **sufficient factual matter**, accepted as true, to "state a claim to relief that is **plausible on its face**."
- **Facial plausibility:** the facts let the court draw a **reasonable inference** that D is liable.
- **Not a probability requirement**, but more than a **sheer possibility**.
- Detailed factual allegations aren't required, but **naked assertions, labels, and conclusions** aren't enough.
- Plausibility belongs at the **motion to dismiss** stage, not just at summary judgment.
- Retired [[Conley v. Gibson|Conley's]] "no set of facts" language.

Applied in: [[Rule 12(b)(6) Motion to Dismiss]] · [[Swanson v. Citibank]] · possibly to [[Affirmative Defenses]] (courts divided)
