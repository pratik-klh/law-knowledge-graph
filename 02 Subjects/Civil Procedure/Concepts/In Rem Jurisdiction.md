# In Rem Jurisdiction

Settles the **title or status of property** within the state.

- After [[Shaffer v. Heitner]], due process analysis applies here too.
- **Pure in rem** cases, where the claim to the property itself is the source of the controversy, will **always** satisfy [[International Shoe Co. v. Washington|International Shoe]]. D's claim to in-state property shows they expected to benefit from the state's protection, so the sovereignty prong is met.
- Compulsory counterclaim exception: no need to bring a counterclaim if the original claim rests on in rem jurisdiction. See [[Counterclaims]].
