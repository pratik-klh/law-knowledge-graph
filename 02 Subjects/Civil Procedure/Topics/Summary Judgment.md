# Summary Judgment (Rule 56)

Very important in practice: summary judgment motions are filed in about **20% of all cases** and granted, at least in part, **more than half the time**.

## Why it exists
A [[Rule 12(b)(6) Motion to Dismiss|12(b)(6)]] motion tests only whether the complaint is sufficient **on its face**. It can't weed out cases where P alleges the elements with some factual detail but **can't prove them**. Summary judgment comes **after discovery**: now that we can see the evidence, a reasonable fact-finder could only rule one way, so there's no need for a jury.

## Rule 56(a)
> "A party may move for summary judgment, identifying each claim or defense—or the part of each claim or defense—on which summary judgment is sought. The court shall grant summary judgment if the movant shows that there is **no genuine dispute as to any material fact** and the movant is **entitled to judgment as a matter of law**."

- If there's no genuine dispute of material fact, there's nothing for the jury to do (juries try disputed facts), so the judge rules for the movant as a matter of law.
- See [[Genuine Dispute of Material Fact]].

## The movant
Either party can move, but **defendants** move far more often. D doesn't bear the burden of proof, so D can just point to an **absence of evidence** on at least one required element. See [[Burden of Production and Persuasion]].

## Standard of review
The motion is viewed **in the light most favorable to the non-moving party**.

## Cases
[[Adickes v. S.H. Kress & Co.]] · [[Celotex Corp. v. Catrett]]

## In practice
1. The movant files the motion with a supporting memo, plus an appendix of everything cited (documents, deposition excerpts, etc.).
2. The non-movant files a memo in opposition, pointing to genuine material disputes that remain (with everything cited attached), or explaining why it can't yet produce that evidence. It must show the facts can be presented through **admissible evidence**.
3. The movant files a reply brief.
4. If SJ is granted on **all** claims, the case is over. If denied, the case goes to **trial**.
