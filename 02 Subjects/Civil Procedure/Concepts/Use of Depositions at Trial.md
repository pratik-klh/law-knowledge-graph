# Use of Depositions at Trial (Rule 32)

A deposition can be used **against a party** who was present, was represented, or had reasonable notice, if consistent with the rules of evidence.

| Rule | Use |
|---|---|
| 32(a)(2) | **Impeach** or contradict a deponent's trial testimony (any purpose the evidence rules allow) |
| 32(a)(3) | Deposition of an **adverse party** (or its officer, director, managing agent, or 30(b)(6) designee): usable for **any purpose** |
| 32(a)(4) | **Unavailable witness**, any purpose, if the witness is dead; **more than 100 miles** away or outside the U.S. (not procured by the party); ill, infirm, aged, or imprisoned; couldn't be subpoenaed; or exceptional circumstances apply |
| 32(a)(5) | **Limits:** can't use it if short notice was given, a protective-order motion was pending, and the deposition went ahead anyway; or if the deponent couldn't get a lawyer and no leave was obtained |
| 32(a)(6) | **Partial use:** the adverse party can require other parts to be read too, "in fairness" |
| 32(a)(7) | Still usable after a **party substitution** |
| 32(a)(8) | Depositions from **earlier actions** are usable if the subject matter and parties (or representatives) are the same |

## Waiver at trial (Rule 32(b), (d))
- **Errors in how it was taken** (form, oath, badgering, surprise videotaping) are waived if not raised **at the deposition**.
- Objections to the **notice, officer's qualifications, or recording** are waived unless promptly served in writing.
- Objections to **preparation or return** (certification, seal, delivery) are waived unless a **motion to suppress** is promptly filed.
