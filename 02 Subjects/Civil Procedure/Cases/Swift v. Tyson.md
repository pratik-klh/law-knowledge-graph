# Swift v. Tyson (1842)

## Holding
Federal courts could look at **general common law** from all states and decide what seemed the best or majority approach. They didn't have to follow the forum state's common law.

## Problems
- Plaintiffs could get **different law** in federal court than in state court in diversity cases.
- A federal court could choose which law to apply, while the state court had to follow its own state's law.
- This led to **uncertainty** about legal standards and gave plaintiffs an **unfair advantage** (forum shopping).
- Federal courts took a power they lack under the Constitution: the power to **create state law**.

> [!example] Hypo
> I (NY) sue Jeff (NJ) in NJ over a NJ car accident. If NJ law is **unfavorable** to me on a key issue, I'd file in **federal** court. If NJ law is **favorable**, I'd file in **state** court.

**Overruled by:** [[Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins]]
