# Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly (2007)

## Holding
- [[Conley v. Gibson|Conley's]] "no set of facts" language shouldn't be read alone to mean any statement revealing the theory of the claim suffices unless factually impossible. It "**has earned its retirement**."
- **Plausibility** belongs at the **motion to dismiss** stage, not just at summary judgment.

(Context: an antitrust conspiracy claim alleging only parallel conduct was not plausible.)

See [[Plausibility Standard]] · Followed by [[Ashcroft v. Iqbal]]
