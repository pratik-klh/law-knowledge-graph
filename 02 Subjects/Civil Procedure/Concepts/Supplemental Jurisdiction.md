# Supplemental Jurisdiction

**Statute:** [[28 USC 1367 - Supplemental Jurisdiction]]

## Core idea (§1367(a))
If a federal court already has original jurisdiction over the main part of a lawsuit, it can also hear **related claims** that form part of the **same Article III case or controversy**, even if those claims couldn't be in federal court on their own. This includes claims involving joinder or intervention of additional parties.

**Test:** do the state and federal claims derive from a [[Common Nucleus of Operative Fact]]? If so, the court has the **power** to hear the whole case ([[United Mine Workers v. Gibbs|Gibbs]]).

## Limits
- **§1367(b):** in a **diversity-only** case, no supplemental jurisdiction over claims by plaintiffs against parties joined under Rules 14, 19, 20, or 24 (or by Rule 19/24 plaintiffs) when that would be inconsistent with §1332. In short, no SJ if adding the party **destroys diversity**.
- **§1367(c):** the court **may decline** if:
  1. The claim raises a **novel or complex** state-law issue
  2. The claim **substantially predominates** over the original-jurisdiction claims
  3. The court has **dismissed all** original-jurisdiction claims
  4. **Exceptional circumstances** give other compelling reasons

## Discretion: Gibbs factors
Judicial economy · avoiding needless state-law decisions (were federal claims dismissed before trial?) · do state issues predominate? · are state issues closely tied to federal policy? · jury confusion?

## Right or discretion?
[[Executive Software North America v. U.S. District Court]]: majority says it's a matter of right (decline only under §1367(c)); dissent says it's always discretionary. **Circuit split.**
