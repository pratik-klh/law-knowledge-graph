# Traditional Bases of Personal Jurisdiction

The four original bases (era of [[Pennoyer v. Neff]]):
1. **Consent**: D appears voluntarily
2. **Presence**: D is found within the state and served with process (see [[Transient Jurisdiction]])
3. **Residence**: D is a resident of the forum state
4. **Property**: D owns property within the forum state (see [[In Rem Jurisdiction]], [[Quasi in Rem Jurisdiction]])

## Problems with the Pennoyer approach
A rule based on in-state service is **clear but narrow**:
- It's hard to hold people accountable for what they do in a state once they leave.
- Cars and other transportation mean much more movement between states.
- Plaintiffs are forced to track down defendants in unfamiliar states and sue them there.

These problems led to [[International Shoe Co. v. Washington]] and the [[Minimum Contacts]] test. All four bases still count as forms of [[General Jurisdiction]], but after [[Shaffer v. Heitner]] they must also satisfy due process (except presence, per [[Burnham v. Superior Court]]).
