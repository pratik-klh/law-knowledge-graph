# Shaffer v. Heitner (1977)

## Holding
The due process analysis ([[Minimum Contacts]], [[International Shoe Co. v. Washington]]) applies to [[In Rem Jurisdiction|in rem]] and [[Quasi in Rem Jurisdiction|quasi in rem]] cases, as well as [[In Personam Jurisdiction|in personam]] cases.

## Where Shaffer changes nothing
1. **Pure in rem**: the claim to the property itself is the source of the controversy. Shoe is always satisfied. D's claim to in-state property shows D expected to benefit from the state's protection, so sovereignty is met.
2. **Quasi in rem where ownership is conceded** but the claim relates to rights and duties growing out of that ownership. Shoe is always satisfied, because the property is in the forum and the claim arose from that contact.

## Significance
Property **alone** (as an unrelated "hook") is no longer automatically enough.
