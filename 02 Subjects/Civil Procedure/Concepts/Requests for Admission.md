# Requests for Admission (Rule 36)

**Purpose:** to identify and narrow the issues for trial.

- Written factual statements the other party must **admit, deny, or object to**. They can cover facts, opinions, the application of law to fact, and the **genuineness of documents** (attach the document or make it available).
- Each statement must be stated **separately**. There's **no default limit** on the number (court or local rules may set one).
- **Response:** **30 days** (the course also notes 45 days if served with the complaint). **Failure to respond means the matter is deemed admitted.**
- **Ways to respond:** admit, deny, partially admit or qualify, or state lack of information after reasonable inquiry. A party can't refuse just because the information is equally available to the requester.
- Responses are **signed but not under oath**. Objections are due by the response deadline.
- If an answer is insufficient, the court can deem the matter admitted or allow an amended answer. If an objection is improper, the court orders an answer.

## Effect
- Admitted matters are **conclusively established** for the case. That's stronger than ordinary admissions, which can be contradicted. Opposing evidence isn't allowed, and the judge instructs the jury that the matter is proven.
- **Withdrawal or amendment** requires court permission, and only if it promotes the merits and doesn't prejudice the other party (prejudice means detrimental reliance).
- Binding **only in the current litigation**.

## Uses
Authenticate documents · clean up after depositions and interrogatories · follow up on denials with an interrogatory asking for the basis
