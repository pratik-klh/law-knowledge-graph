# Impleader (Rule 14)

> Rule 14(a): "A defending party may, as third-party plaintiff, serve a summons and complaint on a nonparty who **is or may be liable to it** for all or part of the claim against it."

## Meaning
- Addresses an outsider who may **share or be legally responsible for** D's liability to P.
- Needs a theory for why, **if D is liable to P**, the third-party defendant must **reimburse** D for some or all of it.
- **Not** "Sue that guy, not me!"
- Must be based on **derivative liability**. Arising from the same transaction or occurrence is **not enough** ([[Jeub v. B-G Foods]]).

## Legal bases for reimbursement
- **Guarantors:** a landlord sues your parents (guarantors on your lease), and they implead you.
- **Subrogation:** an insurer pays a company on an employee-theft policy, then goes after the thief.
- **Contribution among joint tortfeasors:** under joint-and-several liability statutes, the defendant sued can seek contribution from the other tortfeasors. **Confirm the state has a statute authorizing it.**

## SMJ caution
[[28 USC 1367 - Supplemental Jurisdiction|§1367(b)]]: in diversity-only cases, no supplemental jurisdiction over **P's** claims against Rule 14 third-party defendants if that would destroy diversity.
