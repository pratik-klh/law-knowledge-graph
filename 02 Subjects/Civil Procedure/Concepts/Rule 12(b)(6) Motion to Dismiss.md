# Rule 12(b)(6) Motion to Dismiss

**Failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted.** It tests the **sufficiency of the complaint** on its face.

## D's argument
The facts as stated don't give rise to a claim, because either:
1. the **law** doesn't provide a claim, or
2. there aren't **enough facts** to support the claim's elements.

## Standard
Take all facts as pleaded as **true**, draw all inferences in favor of the pleader, and ask whether they support a cause of action. In practice, the court:
1. **Disregards** purely conclusory allegations
2. Looks at the well-pleaded **factual** allegations, assumes their truth, and decides whether they **plausibly** show entitlement to relief ([[Plausibility Standard]])

> [!tip] Exam
> There are often arguments on both sides. Make the best arguments both ways.

## Limits
If the complaint pleads the elements with plausible facts, the motion is denied and the case goes to [[Discovery]]. It can't weed out claims P won't be able to **prove**; that's the job of [[Summary Judgment]].
