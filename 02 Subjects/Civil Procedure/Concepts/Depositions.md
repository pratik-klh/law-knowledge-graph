# Depositions

A **deposition** is a witness's sworn out-of-court testimony, recorded in writing (usually by a court reporter), for later use in court or for discovery.

**Five kinds:** oral · written · discovery · to preserve testimony · to perpetuate testimony

## Oral depositions (Rule 30)
- A party may depose **any person**, including a party, without leave of court (with the exceptions below). Non-parties are compelled to attend by **subpoena** (Rule 45).
- **Leave of court required** (absent a stipulation) if:
  - it would mean **more than 10 depositions per side**;
  - the deponent has **already been deposed** in the case;
  - it's **before the 26(d) timing**, unless the notice certifies the deponent is about to leave the U.S. and be unavailable; or
  - the deponent is **confined in prison**.
- **Notice:** reasonable written notice to every other party, giving the time and place, the deponent's name and address (or a description if unknown), and the recording method. It can be paired with a [[Requests for Production|Rule 34]] document request.
- **Presumptive limit:** **1 day of 7 hours** per deponent.
- **Organizational deponent (30(b)(6)):** the notice names the organization and lists topics with reasonable particularity. The organization designates who testifies for it.

## Written depositions (Rule 31)
- No parties or attorneys are present. The officer reads written questions and records the answers.
- Same leave-of-court exceptions and notice rules as oral depositions.
- Questions are served in order: direct, cross, redirect, recross.

## Other types
- **Discovery** depositions find facts.
- **Preservation / perpetuation** depositions (e.g., Rule 27, before an action or pending appeal) capture testimony for later use, such as when a witness may become unavailable.
